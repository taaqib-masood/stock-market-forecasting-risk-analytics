📖 How to Read FII/DII Data
▼
⚠️ FII Selling > 3 Days
Usually signals caution. Watch if DII is absorbing the selling — if yes, market holds up. If DII also pulls back, prepare for a correction.
✅ DII > FII Outflow
Domestic institutions (mutual funds, LIC) absorbing foreign selling = strong floor. This is India's structural advantage over other EMs.
💡 Combined Net Flow
When FII + DII net is positive, money is entering markets. Sustained 5+ days of positive net = strong uptrend signal.
📅 F&O Expiry Effect
FII flows tend to be volatile in the week of NSE F&O expiry (last Thursday). Don't over-react to single-day spikes around expiry.

📖 How the graded band works
▼
🟢 Above −8% — fully invested
Ordinary dips aren't worth timing. Stay 100% in the basket so you never miss the snap-back recoveries that punish market-timers.
🟡 −8% to −20% — de-risking
Exposure scales down linearly as the drawdown deepens. Each step to cash is a graded response, not a panic switch — avoiding the whipsaw of a binary on/off rule.
🔴 Below −20% — 30% floor
Catastrophic-crash mode: cut to the 30% floor as insurance. Never zero — long-only, and trying to call the exact bottom is a loser's game.

📚 Scholarly Opinions on Stock Zakat
▼
Method 1 — On Market Value (Majority view)
Pay 2.5% on the current market value of your entire portfolio if held for 1 lunar year (hawl). This is the more conservative and widely followed opinion.
Method 2 — On Gains Only (Minority view)
Some scholars say pay 2.5% only on profits (capital gains), not the principal. This applies if you are a trader (buy/sell frequently) rather than a long-term investor.
Method 3 — Zakatable Assets Ratio
For each company, calculate the proportion of Zakatable assets (cash + receivables) in their balance sheet. Apply 2.5% only to that proportion. Complex but most precise.
Our Recommendation
Use Method 1 for simplicity. It is the opinion of AAOIFI and most contemporary scholars. Consult your local scholar if your circumstances are complex.
📅 Hawl (Lunar Year Condition)
▼

Zakat is only due if your wealth has been above the nisab threshold for a complete lunar year (354 days). If your portfolio dropped below nisab at any point during the year, the hawl resets — you start counting again from when it rises above nisab.

Nisab in 2026: Silver nisab ≈ ₹45,000 (612.36g × ₹73.5/g). Gold nisab ≈ ₹5,85,000 (87.48g × ₹6,686/g). Most scholars use the silver nisab as it catches more people — and your portfolio value of ₹1,24,300 is well above it.
